## Start here

Use this book when you want a quick picture of everyday care at T.L.C. Footprints.

**Know these three things first:**

1. Breakfast and two snacks are provided. Families provide lunch.
2. Learning happens through play, conversation, movement, and dependable routines.
3. Families and the provider share updates that affect the child's day.

Age-specific supplies, feeding, sleep, and care instructions are confirmed before the first day.

## A familiar home setting

T.L.C. Footprints is a licensed California Family Child Care Home in Elk Grove. Care takes place in a home environment where children can build a consistent relationship with their caregiver.

The program is intentionally personal. Current availability depends on children's ages, requested schedules, and California licensing requirements.

Families can review the public license record for facility [**394501929**](https://www.ccld.dss.ca.gov/carefacilitysearch/FacDetail/394501929).

## Meals and daily supplies

T.L.C. Footprints provides:

- Breakfast
- AM snack
- PM snack

Families provide lunch. Food needs, allergies, safe preparation, storage, and age-appropriate feeding instructions should be discussed before enrollment.

Families may also need to provide diapers, wipes, extra clothing, an approved blanket or comfort item when age appropriate, sunscreen with authorization, and a water bottle. The final list depends on the child's age and care needs.

## Play-based and child-led care

Young children learn through play. Building, pretending, moving, sorting, talking, drawing, and repeating an activity all support development.

Child-led does not mean that children decide every part of the day. It means the caregiver notices children's interests and uses them to invite learning within dependable routines and clear safety boundaries.

The adult's role includes preparing the environment, observing, asking useful questions, modeling language, supporting conflict resolution, and extending an activity when a child is ready.

## Readiness grows through ordinary experiences

Early learning is larger than worksheets or memorization.

Daily play can support:

- Language through conversation, songs, stories, and pretend play
- Early math through counting, sorting, patterns, and comparison
- Motor skills through movement, art, building, and self-help tasks
- Social development through taking turns, expressing needs, and repairing conflict
- Confidence through making choices and trying again

Ask what children actually do during the day and how the caregiver adapts activities for different ages.

## Communication with families

Before enrollment, families should understand how everyday updates are shared and which situations lead to immediate contact.

Useful communication includes information that affects the child's care, such as feeding, sleep, toileting, mood, injuries, medication, or an unusual change in behavior.

Families should also share changes in sleep, health, pickup plans, or home routines when they may affect the day. Clear communication works best when both the family and provider know what information is important.

## Infant routines and safe sleep

Infant care requires individual information about feeding, sleep, comfort, and development. Home and child care routines may not be identical, but the plan should be safe, responsive, and workable in licensed group care.

California has specific safe-sleep requirements for licensed child care. Infants are placed on their backs for sleep unless a licensed physician provides a written medical exemption. Sleep spaces remain clear of loose blankets, pillows, stuffed animals, and other soft objects.

An Individual Infant Sleeping Plan is required for infants under 12 months. Families can review [California's official safe-sleep information](https://www.cdss.ca.gov/inforesources/child-care-licensing/public-information-and-resources/safe-sleep) before enrollment.

## What families should understand before starting

Before the first day, make sure you know:

1. Your child's approved schedule and drop-off window
2. What food and supplies must be sent
3. How belongings should be labeled
4. How updates are shared
5. The illness and return-to-care policy
6. Closure dates and pickup expectations
7. Who to contact when plans change

The detailed policies are reviewed during enrollment. If any expectation is unclear, ask before care begins.
