## The enrollment path

### 1. Tour the home

Tours are by appointment. This gives us time to see the environment, talk about your child, and discuss the schedule your family needs without rushing.

### 2. Reserve and enroll

A **{{DEPOSIT}} non-refundable enrollment deposit** is required to reserve your child's childcare space. We then complete the required forms, emergency contacts, and immunization records together.

### 3. Prepare for the first day

We agree on a start date and review what your child needs for a comfortable beginning. Age-specific care instructions and supplies are confirmed before care starts.

## A practical packing list

Every item should be labeled with your child's name.

Most families should plan for:

<Checklist
  id="enroll-packing"
  items={[
    "Lunch in the format reviewed with the provider",
    "A complete change of clothing",
    "Weather-ready layers",
    "Diapers, wipes, and authorized cream when used",
    "A labeled water bottle when requested",
    "An approved comfort item when appropriate",
  ]}
/>

Do not place medication, sunscreen, diaper cream, or another topical product in a child's bag without discussing authorization and safe handoff first.

## Infant items to confirm

Infant needs are reviewed individually. A typical list may include labeled bottles, milk or formula, diapers and wipes, two clothing changes, pacifiers when used, and written feeding information.

California requires an Individual Infant Sleeping Plan for infants under 12 months in licensed family child care homes. Soft objects, loose blankets, pillows, and stuffed animals do not belong in an infant sleep space.

Review the [California safe-sleep resources](https://www.cdss.ca.gov/inforesources/child-care-licensing/public-information-and-resources/safe-sleep) and discuss any sleep questions before care begins.

## Make drop-off warm and clear

A short, predictable goodbye is usually easier to understand than leaving without saying goodbye or returning several times.

Try a simple routine:

<Checklist
  id="enroll-drop-off"
  items={[
    "Arrive with enough time to complete a calm handoff.",
    "Share any important update with the provider.",
    "Use the same short goodbye phrase.",
    "Let the caregiver take over.",
    "Follow the update plan agreed on before the first day.",
  ]}
/>

Your child may cry and still settle after you leave. A difficult goodbye does not automatically mean the entire day will be difficult.

## Expect adjustment to change from day to day

The first day may feel easy because everything is new. A later day may feel harder once the new routine becomes familiar.

At home, protect time for rest and connection. Keep morning routines simple, and avoid promising that a child will not feel sad. A more helpful message is that they are safe, their caregiver will help them, and you will return.

Parents adjust too. Agreeing on when an update is realistic can reduce worry without interrupting active supervision.

## Keep communication useful

Tell the provider about changes that may affect the day, such as poor sleep, medication, a difficult morning, or a change in pickup plans.

Ask for a conversation when distress is intense, continues for long periods, or does not gradually improve. The goal is not a fixed adjustment deadline. The goal is a shared understanding of what the child is experiencing and what support may help.
