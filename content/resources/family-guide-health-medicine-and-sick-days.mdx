## Start here

Use this book before your child becomes sick or needs medication during care.

**Prepare these three things now:**

1. Current health information and written instructions when needed.
2. A parent or approved backup who can pick up promptly.
3. A medication and allergy plan agreed on before drop-off.

Confirm T.L.C. Footprints' current illness and return-to-care policy during enrollment. Do not wait for a sick morning to ask how it works.

## Share health information before care

Tell the provider about any health information that could affect your child's care, including:

- Allergies and known reactions
- Current medication
- Asthma, seizures, or another ongoing condition
- Feeding or mobility needs
- Recent illness or exposure that may affect the group
- Signs that usually mean your child is becoming unwell

Ask what written instructions or health care provider documentation may be needed. Update the information whenever the plan changes.

## Make the morning decision early

Check how your child feels before leaving home. A child may need to stay home when a new illness prevents meaningful participation or requires more care than the provider can safely give while supervising the group.

California provides symptom-based guidance for child care and school. Review the [official CDPH illness guidance](https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CID/DCDC/Pages/Schools/SymptomGuidance.aspx), then confirm T.L.C. Footprints' current illness and return-to-care policy. The program policy and instructions for a diagnosed illness may be more specific.

When uncertain, contact the provider before arrival. A short conversation is easier than arranging pickup immediately after drop-off.

## If symptoms begin during care

Make sure the provider can reach a parent, guardian, and at least one dependable local backup. Decide in advance who can leave work or another commitment when the child needs prompt pickup.

When contacted:

1. Confirm who is coming.
2. Give a realistic arrival time.
3. Share any important medical direction.
4. Ask what symptoms were observed and when they began.
5. Follow up before the child returns.

Call 911 for an emergency. Do not use the website or a routine message when immediate medical help is needed.

## Handle medication before drop-off

Never leave prescription medicine, over-the-counter medicine, sunscreen, diaper cream, or another health product in a child's bag without discussing it first.

Ask the provider what authorization, instructions, packaging, and safe handoff are required. California publishes a parent consent form for medication administration within its [official child care forms list](https://www.cdss.ca.gov/inforesources/forms-brochures/forms-alphabetic-list/i-l).

Before care, confirm:

- The exact product and child's name
- The dose, time, and reason it is used
- Storage requirements
- Start and end dates
- What should happen if a dose is missed or a reaction occurs

The provider must agree that the requested plan can be carried out safely in the child care setting.

## Prepare for allergies and urgent needs

For a serious allergy or ongoing condition, provide a clear written plan from the appropriate health care professional when requested. Review symptoms, response steps, emergency medication, storage, and when to call 911.

Every adult authorized to act should know which information has changed. Update emergency contacts and medication details immediately, not at the next annual review.

Keep a second copy of essential instructions for your own records. Ask how substitute care, field activities, or an evacuation would affect the plan.

## Build a backup care plan

Illness, provider closures, and family emergencies can interrupt the usual schedule. Identify more than one person who may be able to help, and confirm that each person is authorized for pickup.

Your plan might include:

- One nearby family member or trusted adult
- A way to divide sick-day coverage between caregivers
- Current work and emergency phone numbers
- Transportation for an early pickup
- A small reserve of leave or flexible work time when possible

California also offers a short [backup child care planning guide](https://www.cdss.ca.gov/inforesources/child-care-and-development/parent-resources/your-back-up-plan).

## A quick health check before the week begins

Take two minutes to confirm:

1. Emergency contacts can still be reached.
2. Medication and health instructions are current.
3. Supplies are labeled and not expired.
4. Your backup pickup person is still available.
5. You know how to report an absence.

Bring questions to T.L.C. Footprints before a health need becomes urgent. A shared plan protects the child, the family, and the rest of the child care group.
