A child care tour can feel like a lot to absorb at once. You are meeting a caregiver, taking in a new environment, and trying to remember every practical question that matters to your family.

Bring the same list to each program. Consistent questions make it much easier to compare your notes afterward.

## Before you go

Review the provider's licensing information and write down anything you want clarified. This guide explains [how to check a California child care license](/resources/how-to-check-a-california-daycare-license).

Also decide which details are non-negotiable for your family: hours, budget, schedule, location, care philosophy, or support for a specific need.

## The twelve questions

### 1. What does a typical day look like?

Ask the provider to walk you through a recent day. Listen for enough detail to understand meals, rest, indoor and outdoor play, transitions, and how the rhythm changes for different ages.

### 2. What happens when you are ill or need time off?

This is especially important in a family child care home. Ask whether there is a qualified substitute, how much notice families usually receive, and what backup care you would need to arrange.

### 3. How many children are in care, and what are their ages?

Ask about the group your child would actually join, not only the licensed maximum. Then compare the provider's stated capacity with the licensing record.

### 4. How do you respond to hitting, biting, or conflict?

Look for a calm, age-appropriate process that protects everyone, helps children regulate, and keeps families informed when needed.

### 5. How do you handle a difficult drop-off?

Ask how the provider comforts a child, communicates with the parent, and decides when a longer conversation or different transition plan is needed.

### 6. How will I hear about my child's day?

Daily conversation, a written note, text, or an app can all work. Clarify what information is shared, how often, and how you will discuss concerns that need more than a quick pickup conversation.

### 7. What is the illness policy?

Ask which symptoms require staying home, when a child may return, what happens if symptoms begin during the day, and who must be available for pickup.

### 8. What are your hours and late-pickup expectations?

Confirm the exact opening and closing times, accepted schedules, late fees, and what to do if an emergency delays you.

### 9. What is included in tuition?

Ask about meals, snacks, diapers, activities, supplies, deposits, and any additional fees. Use this guide to compare [the full cost of child care](/resources/what-home-daycare-costs-in-elk-grove), not only the weekly rate.

### 10. Is tuition based on enrollment or attendance?

Many programs charge for the reserved space even when a child is absent. Ask how vacations, illness, holidays, provider closures, and schedule changes affect tuition.

### 11. Who lives or works in the home?

For family child care, ask whether there is an assistant or substitute, who else may be present, and how the provider meets California's background-clearance requirements. This is also the time to ask about pets, smoking, and areas of the home used for care.

### 12. May I review your current licensing and safety information?

Ask to see current licensing information, emergency plans, and any policies that matter to your child. For an infant, ask to see the sleep space and safe-sleep plan. If you want references, ask whether any current or former families have agreed to be contacted.

## What to notice during the visit

The conversation matters, but so does the environment around it.

**Notice how the space is used.** Can children move, play, rest, and eat safely? Are materials appropriate for the ages in care?

**Listen to the provider's language.** Warmth, patience, clear boundaries, and respect are easier to hear in ordinary conversation than in a prepared description.

**Look at supervision.** Consider sight lines, gates, outlets, cleaning products, outdoor security, and safe-sleep spaces for infants.

**Notice the pace.** A calm program can still be active and noisy. What matters is whether the provider appears attentive and the environment feels manageable.

**Pay attention to communication.** Do you receive direct answers? Does the provider ask thoughtful questions about your child and family too?

If children are present during the tour, respect their privacy. Some providers schedule tours outside care hours or limit which areas visitors enter while children are in care.

## After the tour

Write down your impressions before the visits begin to blur together. Note both practical facts and how the conversation felt.

Then compare the program with your family's actual needs. A beautiful room cannot solve a schedule that does not work, and a perfect checklist cannot replace trust in the person caring for your child.
