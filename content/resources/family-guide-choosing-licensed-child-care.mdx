## Start here

Use this book when you are comparing child care providers.

**If you only have ten minutes:**

1. Check the provider's license number and public reports.
2. Save the tour checklist before your visit.
3. Compare the full schedule, cost, communication, and care experience.

You do not need to make a decision during the tour. Take notes, then review them when you have time to think.

## Begin with the California license search

The California Department of Social Services publishes information about licensed child care facilities.

**[Open the official facility search](https://www.ccld.dss.ca.gov/carefacilitysearch/)**

Ask each provider for the exact facility number. A small family child care home may be difficult to find through a broad location search, so the number is the most reliable starting point.

T.L.C. Footprints' facility number is [**394501929**](https://www.ccld.dss.ca.gov/carefacilitysearch/FacDetail/394501929).

## Know what the record can tell you

Depending on the facility, the public record may show:

- License status and facility type
- Licensed capacity
- Inspection and visit reports
- Violations, citations, and correction information
- Complaint investigation reports when available

Open the reports and read the details. A number by itself does not explain what happened, whether safety was affected, how the concern was corrected, or whether the same issue happened again.

## Know what the record cannot tell you

California notes that its online search contains a limited period of information and should not be the only basis for a decision. Contact the regional licensing office listed on the record if you need additional history or clarification.

A license also cannot tell you whether the environment feels warm, whether communication works for your family, or whether your child connects with the caregiver. Those questions require a visit and a direct conversation.

## Bring a focused tour checklist

These questions reveal more than a long list of generic prompts:

1. What does a typical day feel like for children?
2. How many children would be with my child, and what are their ages?
3. How do you handle difficult drop-offs, conflict, hitting, or biting?
4. How will I hear about my child's day?
5. What illness symptoms require a child to stay home?
6. What are the hours, closure dates, and late-pickup expectations?
7. What is included in tuition, and what must families provide?
8. Is tuition based on enrollment or attendance?
9. Who lives or works in the home?
10. May I review current licensing and safety information?

Use the same core questions with every provider. Consistent questions make comparison much easier afterward.

## Compare the setting, not only the label

A licensed family child care home and a licensed child care center can both provide excellent care. Focus on the experience your child would actually have.

Compare:

- The size and age mix of the group
- How many caregivers your child sees during a normal week
- The plan for provider illness, time off, or staffing changes
- Daily routines, learning opportunities, and outdoor time
- Communication with families
- Total cost, including deposits, meals, supplies, closures, and fees

If possible, visit more than one type of setting. Notice how adults speak with children, how children move through the space, and whether the program's written policies match the conversation.

## A simple decision check

Before enrolling, make sure you can answer yes to these questions:

- I verified the license and reviewed the available reports.
- I understand the schedule, cost, closures, and attendance policy.
- I know how the provider communicates about my child's day.
- I understand what happens when my child or the provider is ill.
- I feel comfortable asking questions and receiving clear answers.
- I can picture my child feeling safe and known in this setting.

When you are ready to see T.L.C. Footprints, [request a tour](/tour). Careful questions are welcome.
