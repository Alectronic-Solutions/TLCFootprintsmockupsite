Every child care program has its own packing, storage, food, and medication policies. Use this as a starting checklist, then confirm the final list with your provider before the first day.

## Start with the daily essentials

### Lunch

At T.L.C. Footprints, breakfast, a morning snack, and an afternoon snack are provided. Families provide lunch.

Ask how food should be packed, whether anything can be heated or refrigerated, and how allergies and dietary needs are handled.

### A complete change of clothes

Pack clothing that fits, suits the weather, and can handle active, messy play. A complete change usually includes a top, pants or shorts, underwear if used, and socks.

Younger children may need more than one change. Check the spare clothing regularly because children outgrow it quickly.

### Weather-ready layers

Ask what your provider wants for outdoor play. Depending on the season, that may include a jacket, sun hat, or other layer.

Do not send sunscreen, insect repellent, or another topical product until you understand the provider's authorization and storage policy.

### An approved comfort item

A familiar photo or small comfort item may help during the transition if the provider allows it.

For infants, comfort items are for awake time only. Blankets, stuffed animals, pillows, and other soft objects never belong in an infant sleep space.

## Infant items to confirm

Ask your provider how much to send, how items should be labeled, and what may be stored at the program.

A typical infant list may include:

- Labeled bottles
- The milk or formula needed for the day
- Diapers and wipes
- At least two complete changes of clothing
- Pacifiers, if used and accepted
- Required feeding information and care forms

California requires an Individual Infant Sleeping Plan for infants under 12 months in licensed family child care homes. Review the [infant care and safe-sleep guide](/resources/infant-care-in-a-home-daycare) before the first day.

## Toddler items to confirm

- Diapers, wipes, and diaper cream if used and authorized
- More than one clothing change
- Shoes or indoor footwear requested by the provider
- A spill-resistant water bottle, if requested

## Preschool-age items to confirm

- At least one complete clothing change
- A jacket or layer the child can practice managing independently
- A labeled water bottle, if requested
- Any approved comfort item needed during the transition

## Label every separate piece

Put your child's name on bottles, bottle caps, containers, clothing, jackets, water bottles, and comfort items, not only on the bag holding them.

Several children may own the same blue cup or the same size jacket. Clear labels reduce mix-ups and make the end-of-day routine easier for everyone.

## What not to send without approval

**Medication or topical products.** Ask about authorization, original packaging, and secure handoff. Never place medicine in a child's bag without telling the provider.

**Food the provider has not approved.** Confirm allergy rules, safe preparation, storage, and age-appropriate food requirements.

**Small toys from home.** They can become lost, damaged, or difficult to share. An approved comfort item is different.

**Valuable or irreplaceable belongings.** Child care includes paint, water, outdoor play, and spills. Choose comfortable items that can get messy.

## What stays and what comes home

Ask where spare clothing and supplies are stored, how low-supply reminders are handled, and what must go home each day.

Items such as lunch containers, bottles, water bottles, soiled clothing, and artwork commonly travel home daily. Spare clothing, diapers, wipes, and approved supplies may stay if the provider has safe storage for them.

## First-day checklist to confirm

Before the first day, make sure you have clear answers about:

1. Lunch, bottles, and other food
2. Clothing changes
3. Diapers, wipes, and authorized creams
4. Approved comfort items
5. Required forms and written care instructions
6. What stays at care and what comes home
7. How every item should be labeled

Pack the bag the night before and place anything refrigerated beside a visible reminder. A calmer morning gives everyone a better start.
