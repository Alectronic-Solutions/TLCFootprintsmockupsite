Leaving a baby with someone new can feel especially hard. Infant care also comes with specific questions about sleep, feeding, comfort, and communication, so it helps to know what to look for before you enroll.

## Safe sleep in a California family child care home

Safe sleep is not a matter of personal preference. California has specific requirements for licensed family child care homes.

For infants up to 12 months old, those requirements include:

- Placing the infant on their back for sleep unless a licensed physician provides a written medical exemption
- Using an approved crib or play yard with a firm mattress and tightly fitted sheet
- Keeping the sleep space free of loose objects, including blankets, pillows, bumpers, and stuffed animals
- Not swaddling an infant while the infant is in care
- Using car seats for transportation rather than routine sleep
- Physically checking a sleeping infant every 15 minutes and documenting those checks
- Staying close enough to hear the infant wake

If an infant can roll from back to stomach and stomach to back, California's Individual Infant Sleeping Plan documents that ability. The infant is still placed on their back at the start of sleep but may remain in another position after rolling independently when the required plan is complete.

Ask to see where your baby will sleep and how the provider documents sleep checks. A clear answer should include the actual sleep space, the monitoring routine, and the Individual Infant Sleeping Plan for babies under 12 months.

You can review the [California Department of Social Services safe-sleep information](https://www.cdss.ca.gov/inforesources/child-care-licensing/public-information-and-resources/safe-sleep) before your tour.

## Feeding and daily rhythms

Babies do not all eat, sleep, or settle at the same times. A provider should learn your baby's current feeding pattern, sleep cues, comfort needs, and any instructions that can be followed safely in care.

Ask how the provider records bottles, food, naps, and diaper changes. Also ask how changes at home, such as starting solids or dropping a nap, are communicated and incorporated into the care plan.

Consistency can help, but home and child care will not be identical. The goal is a safe, responsive plan that respects your baby's needs and works in a licensed group-care setting.

## What families commonly send

Every provider has a specific policy, so confirm quantities and storage instructions before the first day. An infant list commonly includes:

- Labeled bottles and the milk or formula needed for the day
- Diapers and wipes
- At least two complete changes of clothing
- Pacifiers, if used and accepted by the provider
- Written feeding information and required care forms

A comfort item may help while a baby is awake if the provider allows it, but blankets, stuffed animals, and other soft items never belong in an infant sleep space.

## Communication to discuss before enrollment

Ask what information you will receive about:

- Bottles, meals, and new foods
- Naps and sleep checks
- Diaper changes
- Mood, comfort, and anything unusual
- Supplies that need to be replenished

The format may be a conversation, written log, text, or app. What matters is agreeing on a method that is clear, realistic, and consistent.

## The transition into care

Some babies settle quickly; others need more time. Progress may be uneven, with an easy day followed by a difficult one. That does not automatically mean the placement is wrong.

Before the first day, agree on the drop-off routine, what comfort strategies the provider uses, and when the provider will contact you. If distress is intense, lasts for long periods, or does not improve, talk openly about what both of you are observing.

## Questions to ask about infant care

Along with [the general tour questions](/resources/questions-to-ask-on-a-daycare-tour), ask:

1. Where will my baby sleep, and may I see the sleep space?
2. How do you follow and document California's safe-sleep requirements?
3. How will we create and update my baby's feeding and sleep plan?
4. How will I hear about bottles, naps, diapers, and mood?
5. How many infants are in care, and what are the ages of the other children?
6. How do you respond when a baby is upset or needs comfort?
7. What supplies do I provide, and how should they be labeled?
