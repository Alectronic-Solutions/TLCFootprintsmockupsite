A family child care home and a child care center are different types of licensed care in California. Neither is automatically better. The right choice depends on the environment, relationships, schedule, and practical support your family needs.

## The licensing difference

**A Family Child Care Home** provides licensed care in the licensee's own residence. California licenses family homes as small or large, with capacity and staffing requirements set by the state.

**A Child Care Center** provides licensed care in a dedicated facility. Centers usually have a director, multiple staff members, and rooms or groups organized by age.

Both types are overseen by the California Department of Social Services, Community Care Licensing Division. Both have licensing information families can review. Use this guide to [check a California child care license](/resources/how-to-check-a-california-daycare-license) before you enroll.

## What may feel different day to day

### Group size and ages

Family child care homes often serve a smaller, mixed-age group. Children may spend the day with babies, toddlers, and preschool-age children rather than only same-age peers.

That can create a family-like rhythm: younger children observe older ones, and older children practice patience and helping. A center may be a better fit if you specifically want a larger group of children at the same developmental stage.

Ask each provider how many children are present, what ages they are, and how the group changes during the day. The license type alone does not tell you what your child's actual group will look like.

### Consistency of caregivers

In many homes, one provider is the child's primary caregiver throughout the day. That can make the relationship feel especially consistent and give families one direct point of contact.

Centers work with larger teams. Staff coverage can make it easier for a center to remain open when one employee is absent, although children may interact with several caregivers.

For a home provider, ask what happens when the licensee is ill, takes time off, or needs emergency coverage. A clear backup plan helps you plan your own work and family responsibilities.

### Hours and scheduling

Some home providers offer flexible arrangements, while others keep firm schedules. Centers also vary widely. Compare the actual opening hours, accepted schedules, late-pickup policy, holidays, and closure days rather than assuming one type will be more flexible.

### Cost

Rates depend on age, schedule, meals, staffing, and what the tuition includes. A home may cost less than a nearby center, but that is not guaranteed.

Compare the complete cost: weekly tuition, deposits, paid closures, meals, supplies, and late fees. This guide explains [which child care costs to compare in Elk Grove](/resources/what-home-daycare-costs-in-elk-grove).

### Learning and daily structure

Centers often use a written curriculum and scheduled activity blocks. Family homes vary from highly structured programs to flexible, play-based care.

Ask what happened during a recent day, how much choice children have, and how the provider adapts activities for different ages. A clear, specific answer matters more than the label a program uses.

## A center may fit your family when

- You need the staffing coverage of a larger team.
- You prefer a larger group of same-age peers.
- You want a particular curriculum, classroom structure, or progress-reporting system.
- Your child needs on-site services or specialists a smaller program does not offer.
- The center's hours and closure calendar fit your schedule better.

## A home may fit your family when

- Your child is comfortable in a smaller, home-like environment.
- You value a consistent relationship with a primary caregiver.
- You like the social experience of a mixed-age group.
- You want direct communication with the person caring for your child.
- The home's schedule, approach, and total cost fit your family.

## How to decide

Tour at least one home and one center if you can. Bring [the same questions](/resources/questions-to-ask-on-a-daycare-tour), take notes, and compare what you actually observe rather than what you expected from each category.

Pay attention to how the adults speak with children, whether the daily rhythm feels calm and purposeful, and whether you can picture having an honest conversation there when something is difficult.
